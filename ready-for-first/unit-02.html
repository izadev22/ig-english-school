Grammar · Gerunds and infinitives

Why this form?

These extracts come from the parkour article. For each underlined word, choose why a gerund, an infinitive without to or an infinitive with to is used.

Example: ... the UK became the first country in the world to recognise parkour as a sport. → the infinitive with to is used after the first + noun, in place of a relative clause (the first country ... which recognised parkour).

The rules in brief

Gerund (-ing): as the subject or object of a sentence (Reading in the car makes me feel sick); after prepositions, including look forward to and get used to; after verbs like admit, avoid, can't help, can't stand, consider, enjoy, feel like, give up, mind, miss, put off, suggest; after expressions like have difficulty (in), it's no use, there's no point (in), it's (not) worth.

Infinitive with to: to express purpose (I'm learning English to get a better job); after adjectives (It's difficult to..., I was surprised to hear...); after nouns like ability, chance, decision, idea, opportunity; after verbs like afford, agree, decide, hope, manage, offer, promise, refuse, seem; after verb + object with advise, allow, encourage, persuade, want, warn; after the first / the last / the only + noun.

Infinitive without to: after modal verbs (can, must, should) and after let, make, would rather, had better.

Both, with a change of meaning: remember/forget (doing = a past action; to do = something you have to do), stop (doing = no longer do it; to do = interrupt one activity to do another), try (doing = an experiment; to do = an attempt), mean (doing = involve; to do = intend), need (doing = passive meaning; to do = active).

Preferences: prefer + gerund or infinitive for general preferences; would prefer + infinitive with to and would rather + infinitive without to for specific occasions (I'd rather walk than go by bus).

    Part 2 · Article

    My favourite sport

    The task: You see this notice in an English-language magazine. ARTICLES WANTED! My favourite sport. Write us an article telling us about your favourite sport. Why do you like it and what advice would you give to someone who wants to take it up? The best articles will be published in this magazine. Write your article in 140–190 words.

    Read the model answer and match each summary (a–d) to a paragraph (1–4).

    A strange way to enjoy yourself

    1 Have you ever seen a smile on the face of a long-distance runner? Running ten kilometres or more certainly doesn't sound much fun, but this sport is a powerful addiction and once you've started, you'll find it difficult to give it up. 2 So what is the attraction of running? For me, whether I'm working or studying, there is no better way of getting rid of stress. I can think through my problems and at the end of the race I have the answers. And simply completing a half or full marathon increases my confidence and makes me feel on top of the world. 3 If you're thinking of taking it up yourself, don't try to do too much at the beginning. You should set yourself realistic targets and always do warm-up exercises before you run. Also, make sure you buy a good pair of running shoes to protect your knees and back from injury. 4 And don't be put off by the expressions on the faces of the runners – they're enjoying every minute, and so will you!

    What makes it an article?

    Match each feature of the model answer to its purpose.

    Contractions: doesn't, you've, you'll, I'm, you're, don't, they're

    Informal linkers: So, And, Also

    Direct questions: Have you ever seen a smile on the face of a long-distance runner? So what is the attraction of running?

    Phrasal verbs: give it up, think through, taking it up, put off

    Part 4 · Multiple choice

    Unusual sports

    You will hear a man called Mike Taylor talking on the radio about unusual sports. For questions 1–7, choose the best answer (A, B or C).

    What to expect: you will hear distractors, so listen carefully both times. An option may be true but still not answer the question: in question 2, all three statements are true, but only one is something Mike finds difficult to believe.

    Worked example, question 1. When Mike saw a chess boxing match, he was surprised by A how skilled the competitors were at both parts of the sport / B how much fun the competitors were having / C how aggressive the competitors were.

    In the recording: "Sounds like just a bit of fun, but when I watched two men competing on German television recently, I was amazed by their level of skill in each of these two very different disciplines. After all, boxing is such an aggressive, physical sport, whereas chess is all about using the brain."

    The answer is A: amazed by their level of skill matches surprised by how skilled. B and C are distractors: "a bit of fun" and "aggressive" describe the sport in general, not what surprised him.

    Part 3 · Collaborative task

    What makes us happy?

    Work in pairs. Here are some things that many people believe are important if we want to feel happy. Talk to each other about how important these things are for our personal happiness.

    How important are these for our personal happiness? listening to good music being physically fit having good friends doing a job you like having a lot of money
    Task 1 · discuss together2:00

    Now decide which two things are most important for our personal happiness.

    Task 2 · decide together1:00
    How to go about it: this is an interactive task. Give your own opinions, but also ask your partner what they think and respond by agreeing, disagreeing or adding a comment, with reasons. Don't start deciding during task 1; in the exam you won't know the second question until you finish it. You don't have to agree in task 2. In the exam you have two minutes for task 1 and one minute for task 2.

    Asking your partner

    • What do you think about ...?
    • How important do you think ... is?
    • Do you agree?

    Agreeing and disagreeing politely

    • That's a good point. / I couldn't agree more.
    • I see what you mean, but ...
    • I'm not so sure about that. For me, ...

    Reaching a decision

    • So, shall we choose ... and ...?
    • I think we should go for ..., because ...
    • We'll have to agree to disagree on that one.
