Grammar · Habitual behaviour

Where does the frequency adverb go?

Frequency adverbs (always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never...) go before the main verb and after an auxiliary verb or be. Some can also start a sentence; others can't.

Correct or incorrect?

Is the adverb in the right position in each sentence?

Grammar · Past habits

Used to or would?

Both describe past habits that don't happen any more, but only one of them works with states (verbs like be, have, like, know).

Rule: would only works with repeated actions. For states (be, have, like, want, know...), use used to.

Repeated action, state or one-off event?

Read the text. For each underlined verb, decide: (a) both used to and would are possible, (b) only used to is possible, or (c) neither is possible, because it's a single event and not a habit.

Grammar · Used to

Be used to, get used to and used to

be used to = already accustomed to something (a state).
get used to = becoming accustomed to something (a process, a change).
used to + infinitive = a past habit that has stopped.

After be/get used to, always use an -ing form or a noun, never an infinitive.

Complete with the correct form

Use used to, be used to or get used to in the right form.

Your turn: informal letter

You have received this letter from your English-speaking friend, Tom. Write your reply in 140–190 words.

"I'm not sure what to do during the summer holidays next year. My parents want me to help out in the shop they own, but I'd quite like to work in a hotel in your country – there are plenty of jobs available. What do you think I should do? Write soon, Tom"

0 words (target: 140–190)

Dear Tom, It was great to hear from you! That's a very important choice to make, and I think you should think carefully about it before choosing. I'm certain you would enjoy working in a hotel and that you would do it very well and, obviously, that would be a great experience and you would earn a great deal of money. However, it's true you would be far from your family and friends and, if you are not used to work many hours, you will find it exhausting. On the other hand, as your parents need help, if you stay, you will be giving them a rest from work and also you will be in your city and in your free time you will be able to hang out with your friends. Because of that, in my opinion the best thing for you to do is to stay at home and help your parents in the shop. You will have more opportunities to travel abroad when you are older. In any case, regardless what you decide, I'm sure you will have a great summer. A hug Lucía

187 words · Mark: good pass

Examiner comments

Content: All the content is relevant and informative. The writer considers both options before advising Tom which to choose.

Communicative achievement: The conventions of letters are used appropriately. The opening and closing comments are generally appropriate, although "A hug" is not usual. The register is consistently informal and the tone friendly and helpful.

Organisation: The letter is well organised into logical paragraphs. A range of linking words is used, particularly at the beginning of sentences (However; On the other hand; Because of that; In any case). However, there is an over-reliance on and to link ideas within sentences.

Language: A reasonable range of everyday language is used (e.g. think carefully; earn a great deal of money; exhausting; have more opportunities to travel abroad; hang out with your friends). Both simple and complex forms are used with good control, and errors do not prevent understanding (you are not used to work working; regardless [of] what you decide).

Five people talk about their clothes

You will hear five short extracts in which people are talking about the clothes they wear. For questions 1–5, choose from the list (A–H) what each speaker says. Use the letters only once. There are three extra letters which you do not need to use.

Before you listen: you won't hear the same words as in sentences A–H, so think of another way of saying each idea. For example, A (I tend to wear the same clothes all the time) could sound like: "I feel really comfortable in a white shirt and jeans, and that's what I wear nearly every day."

  3. I refuse to wear designer clothes.
  4. I'm careful to check the origin of the clothes I buy.
  5. I generally wear second-hand clothes.
  6. I only throw clothes away when absolutely necessary.
  7. I buy quality clothes that are guaranteed to last.
  8. I wear clothes that give me self-confidence.

Track 1.3

Many English speakers join words together when they speak quickly. In questions with do/did + you, it can be difficult to hear whether the speaker is saying do or did:

What do you want to do? /wɒdʒə wɒnə duː/
How did you travel? /haʊdʒə trævəl/

The context usually makes it clear whether the speaker means do or did, even when the difference is hard to hear.

Write down the four questions you hear

Listen and type each question. Listen as many times as you need.

Track 1.4

Now say them

Work in pairs. First say the four questions as clearly as possible. Then say them again, joining the words together as in the recording.

Stress the information words (question words, nouns and main verbs) and keep the small grammar words weak: Where did you go this morning? The last important word usually gets the strongest stress.

In the exam: Pronunciation is one of the marking criteria in the Speaking paper. The examiner asks: Are the answers clear, and can the speaker be generally understood? Is the intonation appropriate? Is sentence stress and word stress correct? Are individual sounds clear and correctly produced?
