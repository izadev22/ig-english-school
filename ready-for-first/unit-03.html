Comparative and superlative forms

Write the comparative and superlative of each word. Example: strange → stranger, the strangest.

The rules in brief

Form: one-syllable adjectives add -er/-est (cheap, cheaper, the cheapest; double the consonant after a short vowel: thin, thinner). Two-syllable adjectives ending in -y change to -ier/-iest (happier). Most other adjectives with two or more syllables, and most adverbs, use more/the most (more sincere, more quietly). Some two-syllable adjectives can do both (cleverer / more clever). Irregular: good/well → better → the best; bad/badly → worse → the worst; far → further/farther → the furthest/farthest.

Small and big differences: a bit, a little, slightly + comparative (slightly bigger); much, (quite) a lot, far, significantly + comparative (much faster). By far / easily + superlative (by far the best). Use in before the group: the naughtiest boy in the class.

as ... as: (just) as + adjective + as for similarity; not (quite / nearly / so) ... as for differences (not nearly as bad as = a big difference). as much + uncountable, as many + countable. the same + noun + as (the same age as my dad).

Other patterns: less/the least are the opposites of more/the most; fewer with plural nouns. The + comparative, the + comparative for changes that happen together (The more money I have, the faster I spend it). There isn't much difference between .... make more of an effort / pose more of a danger.

Articles: the rules in brief

the: when there is only one (the manager); when something is mentioned again; when both people know what you mean (The film starts in 10 minutes); for a specific part of something (the beginning of the film); for groups like inventions and animal species (The mop was invented in Spain); with adjectives for groups of people (the homeless, the rich); with superlatives; with musical instruments (play the piano); with transport on a timetable (get the bus); with some countries (the USA), oceans, mountain ranges, deserts and rivers.

a/an: when you first mention a singular countable noun; for any one of several things (Could you pass me a biscuit?); for jobs (She's a nuclear scientist); with some numbers (a hundred, a thousand); meaning per (twice a day, 50 miles an hour).

No article: for plural or uncountable nouns in general (Do you think computers will replace teachers?); with most streets, towns, cities and countries; in common expressions like go home, be at work/school, in hospital, go by car, on holiday, have breakfast, at night.

Part 1 · Essay

Has technology made a positive contribution to sport today?

The task: Your English class has been discussing the effect of technology on sport. Now your English teacher has asked you to write an essay. Write an essay using all the notes and giving reasons for your point of view. Notes — write about: 1 the use of technology to improve performance; 2 the use of technology in refereeing decisions; 3 ... (your own idea).

1 Some people feel that technology has helped to improve our enjoyment of sport and they approve of its use. Others, however, believe it has a negative effect and would prefer to see its influence limited. 2 On the positive side, the use of technology in football to decide if a goal has been scored or not, or in tennis to judge whether the ball is in or out, clearly helps to make the games fairer. Communication between sports officials via microphones also improves the quality of their decisions. Another positive influence of technology is its role in increasing safety for competitors. Modern helmet design in cycling is an example of this. 3 On the negative side, the use of technology to improve performance can give some sportspeople an unfair advantage over others. For this reason, full-length swimsuits and so-called 'superbikes' have now both been banned from some swimming and cycling competitions. 4 In conclusion, I think that as long as sporting achievements remain the result of human effort, rather than scientific advances, then the contribution of technology will always be positive.

Your turn: essay

Your English class has been discussing the role of information and communications technology in today's world. Now your English teacher has asked you to write an essay. Write an essay using all the notes and giving reasons for your point of view.

Do we really need the internet to enjoy life to the full? Notes — write about: 1 friendships; 2 entertainment; 3 ... (your own idea).

Choosing your own idea: consider how technology does and doesn't help us enjoy life. Reference material: quick, easy access to information gives us more time for leisure. Shopping: simple, with a wider range of products, but perhaps less fun than going to shops with friends. Working at home: more time with family, but it can be lonely and boring. Health: a sedentary lifestyle, back problems, eyesight.
How to go about it: for each note, list positive and negative ideas (friendships — positive: we can be in constant contact; negative: we might not develop good social skills, and it's no substitute for face-to-face conversation). Choose the points you want to include, then plan your introduction and conclusion. Use paragraphs and linking devices, a range of vocabulary and grammar, and a consistently formal or neutral style. Check your grammar, spelling and punctuation at the end.

181 words · Mark: pass

Examiner comments

Content: All content is relevant and the reader is on the whole informed, though not fully. The student refers to both friendships and entertainment, but the latter point is not fully developed. Shopping is the student's own idea. A clear conclusion says that computers and phones are no substitute for contact with real people, although they can be useful.

Communicative achievement: There is a clear essay structure: an opening statement, two paragraphs on the pros and cons of the internet, and a concluding paragraph with the writer's opinion. The register is consistently neutral, with some more formal language to introduce ideas (On the one hand; On the other hand; To conclude; However).

Organisation: Clear paragraphs and a reasonable selection of linking words, particularly across paragraphs. The student probably means as for entertainment when writing about the entertainment.

Language: The errors do not stop communication, but there are so many of them that they distract the reader, especially with verb forms (advantages for have it; for to enjoy; better that go) and articles (enjoy the life; chat on the social media; the games or the films). Some complex structures and less common vocabulary are used successfully (it is much better to speak with your friends personally; get in touch with them online).
